## Verified facts
<!-- Move items here once sourced. Include: claim, source (book chapter, or URL/page). -->
- Ubik was published in 1969. — Wikipedia, "Ubik".
- In 2009 *Time* put it on its list of the 100 best English-language novels since 1923. Lev Grossman
  called it a deeply unsettling existential horror story you can never be sure you've woken up from.
  — Wikipedia, "Ubik".
- In 1974 French filmmaker Jean-Pierre Gorin paid Dick to write a screenplay. It was never filmed. It
  was published in 1985 and again in 2008. — Wikipedia, "Ubik"; philipdick.com.
- Dick's wife Tessa Dick said Ubik is a metaphor for God. — Wikipedia, "Ubik".
- Stanisław Lem praised Dick in "Philip K. Dick: A Visionary Among the Charlatans" (*Science Fiction
  Studies* #5, 1975), and wrote an afterword to the Polish translation. — dynamicsubspace.net; depauw.edu/sfs.
- Celluloid Dreams bought the film rights in 2008. Michel Gondry was announced as director in 2011,
  then left the project after a script draft disappointed him. — Film Stories.
- Book facts (setting June 1992; ads open every chapter; ch. 17 epigraph; Joe Chip coins) — the book,
  chapter refs in plot.md.

## Critical landscape
<!-- Common readings, author background, what other analyses miss. Synthesize; never copy. -->
- Most readings agree the ending is deliberately open: either Runciter is also in half-life, or the
  levels of reality never end. (SuperSummary, ch. 14–17 analysis.)
- The God reading (Ubik as grace; ch. 17 epigraph modelled on the Gospel of John's "In the beginning
  was the Word") is common.
- Less common angle we can use: the book as a satire of consumer culture, where even God arrives as
  a product with a disclaimer.
- Game link: Ubik has inspired games (including a 1998 PC game). Optional aside; verify before use.
