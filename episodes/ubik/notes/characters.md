**Big visual note for the whole cast (ch. 3–8):** Dick dresses 1992 in absurd, clashing "future
fashion" — dirndls with cowboy chaps, shift dresses, propeller beanies, birch-bark pantaloons.
It is a running joke in the book and a gift for the visuals. Everyone in the "real" 1992 should
look gloriously mismatched; in the regressed 1939 world they end up in plain period clothes (ch. 10).

**Book inconsistency to know about:** Ella's eyes are "brown" in ch. 1 (Runciter, from memory)
and "bright and luminous blue" in ch. 2 and ch. 16. Use blue (the book's majority).

## Joe Chip
- **Video role:** main (protagonist).
- **Who he is:** Runciter Associates' field tester — he measures psi and anti-psi fields with his
  gear and writes the hire/don't-hire verdicts, using secret symbols only he and Runciter know
  (ch. 1, 3). Becomes acting head of the firm after the blast (ch. 6–7).
- **Look from the book:** the text gives almost no face. Hungover, haggard, "glum" (ch. 4); feels
  "squat, oily, sweating" next to Wendy (ch. 5); after the Zurich hotel he looks "a hundred years
  older" (ch. 8). **Ours:** thirties, tired face, stubble, rumpled.
- **Clothing / props:** at home: pinstripe clown-style pajamas (ch. 3). Dressed: sporty maroon
  wrapper, turned-up "twinkle-toes" shoes, felt cap with a tassel; a spotty blue greatcape (ch. 3).
  Test gear (drums, needles, polygraph tapes) (ch. 3, 6). Smokes constantly (ch. 3, 9 — he insists
  on cheap Wings). In 1939: knickers, camel's-hair coat, golf cap; or a pinstripe double-breasted
  suit (ch. 10). Has a hovercar (ch. 3).
- **Quirks / habits:** chronically broke — credit rating "quadruple G", voided credit keys, can't
  pay his own coin-operated door (ch. 3); runs on stimulants (ch. 3); pedantic ("farther", "All ions
  are negative", ch. 13, 16); has hunches rather than reasons (ch. 5); a "will to fail", per Al (ch. 7).
- **Arc:** loser → reluctant leader → the last survivor of the group → Ella's chosen successor as
  Runciter's half-life adviser (ch. 16) → his face appears on Runciter's coins (ch. 17).

## Glen Runciter
- **Video role:** main.
- **Who he is:** owner of Runciter Associates, a prudence organization (anti-psi security firm) in
  New York; consults his half-lifer wife Ella in every crisis (ch. 1–2).
- **Look from the book:** tall, elderly, big and massively built; "massive, sloppy head", head
  "massive like a tomcat's" thrust forward; ruffled gray mass of wirelike hair; slightly protruding,
  round, warm, alert eyes; large wide hands; quick sprightly stride; booming voice; wrinkled,
  wattled face (ch. 1, 5). Age unclear — "four times as old" as 20-year-old Ella; possibly kept going
  by artificial organs (ch. 1–2).
- **Clothing / props:** varicolored Dacron wash-and-wear suit, knit cummerbund, dip-dyed cheesecloth
  cravat (ch. 1); a silk sash with a round gold antique pocket watch (ch. 5); green all-Havana cigars
  (ch. 5); amphetamine tablets (ch. 5, 14); a walnut-and-leather swivel chair he loves (ch. 4).
- **Quirks:** always smiling and chuckling on the outside, inwardly remote (ch. 1); writes his own
  ads (ch. 4); in half-life-world "manifestations" he talks like a TV pitchman (ch. 10).
- **Arc:** apparently killed by the bomb on Luna (ch. 6) → his face spreads over money, matchbooks,
  graffiti, notes (ch. 8–12) → reveals he is alive and the others are in cold-pac (ch. 14) → finds
  Joe Chip coins in his own pocket (ch. 17).

## Patricia "Pat" Conley
- **Video role:** main (the apparent villain; red herring).
- **Who she is:** new recruit with a unique talent — she changes the past by thinking about it
  (ch. 3). Parents are precogs working for Hollis (ch. 3). Claims to be Joe's mistress (ch. 7);
  confesses to working with G. G. Ashwood and Hollis (ch. 13).
- **Look from the book:** 17–19 (ch. 3); slim, long-legged, copper-skinned; large, intense black
  eyes; brilliant, tumbling, shiny black hair; sensual, dark lips; a "heavy, sullen fire" beauty;
  tanned, competent arms (ch. 3–4).
- **Clothing / props:** canvas workshirt, jeans, heavy mud-caked boots, hair tied back with a red
  bandanna; knife, field telephone and rations on an imitation-leather belt (ch. 3). Forearm tattoo
  **CAVEAT EMPTOR** (ch. 3). The silver-and-jade wedding ring she kept from an erased timeline
  (ch. 5). On Luna: synthetic sleeveless blouse, torn (ch. 6). A lace-edged handkerchief, cigarettes
  (ch. 13).
- **Quirks:** contemptuous laugh; neutral, devastating put-downs; follows Joe up the stairs, counting
  steps and mocking him (ch. 13).
- **Arc:** hired as a "hazard" (Joe's secret symbol, ch. 3) → shows her power by rewriting the
  morning (ch. 5) → says her talent stopped working (ch. 12) → taken as the cause of everything
  (ch. 12–13) → actually eaten by Jory, "terribly surprised" (ch. 15).

## Ella Runciter (Ella Hyde Runciter)
- **Video role:** main (the helper; the source of Ubik).
- **Who she is:** Runciter's wife, died at 20, kept in half-life in a cold-pac casket at the Beloved
  Brethren Moratorium in Zurich; co-owner of the firm by her will (ch. 1–2).
- **Look from the book:** in the casket: upright in a transparent casket in icy mist, eyes shut,
  hands raised toward her face; "pretty and light-skinned"; blue eyes (ch. 2; see the inconsistency
  note above). In the half-life world (ch. 16): a pretty girl with gay blond pigtails, unbuttoned
  sweater over a blouse, bright red skirt, high-heeled little shoes; warm, intelligent blue eyes;
  rich, warm laugh.
- **Quirks:** dreams of a smoky red light (a bad rebirth, per the Tibetan Book of the Dead) and laughs
  about it (ch. 2, 16); "a selfish, practical reason" for saving Joe (ch. 16).
- **Arc:** her voice is drowned out by Jory (ch. 2) → revealed as the force behind Ubik, with other
  half-lifers (ch. 16) → hands her role to Joe before rebirth (ch. 16).

## Jory Miller
- **Video role:** main (the true antagonist).
- **Who he is:** a half-lifer who died at 15, kept next to Ella; unusually strong; "eats" the
  remaining life of other half-lifers and builds the fake world they experience (ch. 2, 15).
  Also uses the names **Matt** and **Bill** — the Hollis "brothers" from everyone's dreams (ch. 5–6, 15).
- **Look from the book (ch. 15):** a gangly adolescent, unpleasantly slender; irregular black-button
  eyes under tangled brows; nothing on his face matches — over-convoluted ears, insect-hard eyes,
  straight hair but curly bristly eyebrows, a too-thin, too-long sharp nose, a deep cleft in the chin;
  gray, shabby, shovel-like teeth; a misshapen, jeering grin; twitching fingers. Clothes from the
  mid-century: white drip-dry shirt, jeans, laceless leather slippers. As "Matt and Bill" in dreams:
  squirrel-like, knob-headed, tufts of reddish hair, blunt shovel teeth (ch. 5).
- **Disguises:** lives inside "Don Denny" (ch. 15), Dr. Taylor, the bald pharmacist with celluloid
  dentures (ch. 16), possibly others.
- **Quirks:** brags; bites like a sheep grinding ("Gahm grau", ch. 15); hopes they will kill Pat for
  fun; "I always do it this way."
- **Arc:** a voice on Ella's line (ch. 2) → unseen destroyer → revealed (ch. 15) → still undefeated.

## Al Hammond
- **Video role:** main or strong supporting (the clearest thinker; dies mid-book).
- **Who he is:** top anti-precog inertial (ch. 5); Joe's anchor after the blast (ch. 6–9).
- **Look from the book:** excessively tall, stoop-shouldered Black man with a gentle expression and an
  elongated face (ch. 5); about 30; gold-capped front teeth decorated with a heart, a club and a
  diamond (ch. 8). Cut over the right eye from the blast (ch. 6).
- **Clothing:** green felt knickers, gray golf socks, badger-hide open-midriff blouse, imitation
  patent-leather pumps (ch. 8).
- **Arc:** makes the list of "wrong" things (ch. 8), finds the graffiti and works out the truth
  (ch. 9), sees the 1910 elevator alone, dies in a dark men's room (ch. 9).

## Wendy Wright
- **Video role:** supporting (first death; the shock image).
- **Who she is:** inertial; Joe's unspoken ideal (ch. 5); says she will marry Joe (ch. 7).
- **Look from the book:** about 25–26 but seemingly ageless; green eyes "like tumbled stones"; calm,
  untroubled, cool; colors "indirectly lit" (ch. 5). Found dead as a tiny mummified husk with a cloud
  of long, wiry black hair (ch. 8).
- **Arc:** "I feel old. I am old" (ch. 6) → found in the Zurich hotel closet (ch. 8).

## The other inertials (background — the "group")
- **Edie Dorn:** young, thin as a beanpole, glasses, straight lemon-yellow hair; cowboy hat, black
  lace mantilla, Bermuda shorts; shy, wispy voice; tracked Melipone (ch. 1, 5). Dies in Des Moines (ch. 12).
- **Francesca "Francy" Spanish:** good-looking older dark woman, "gypsy-like", unsettling eyes; silk sari,
  nylon obi, bobby socks; long braids (scorched, ch. 6); hears "voices"; dreams the hand of God holding
  an Ubik can (ch. 5, 11).
- **Tippy Jackson:** anti-telepath, thirtyish, mannish, sand-colored; ersatz vicuna trousers, gray
  sweatshirt with a faded Bertrand Russell portrait; married; sleeps till noon with a dream electrode;
  dreams of "Matt and Bill" (ch. 5).
- **Jon Ild:** arrogant woolly-haired teenage boy; floral muumuu and Spandex bloomers; memorizes license
  plates in dreams; carries a laser tube (ch. 5–6).
- **Tito Apostos:** bald with a goatish beard; hip-hugging gold lamé trousers, kelp-green blouse with
  egg-sized buttons; dignified; carries an old lead-slug pistol (ch. 5–6).
- **Don Denny:** slender, earnest, sits bolt upright; baritone; polyester dirndl, long hair in a snood,
  cowboy chaps with silver stars, sandals; horn-rimmed glasses (ch. 5, 15). The only anti-animator.
  **Twist:** the Don Denny of the later chapters is Jory in disguise (ch. 15).
- **Sammy Mundo:** weak-nosed young man with a small melon-shaped head, in a maxiskirt; arrested
  development; strong anti-telepath; giggles (ch. 5). The only one not in cold-pac — in a coma in a
  Zurich hospital (ch. 14).
- **Fred Zafsky:** flabby, big-footed, middle-aged, pasted-down hair, muddy skin, protruding Adam's
  apple; a shift dress "the color of a baboon's ass"; squeaky voice, long arms, sniggers;
  anti-parakineticist (ch. 5, 11).
- **Ours (suggestion):** show them as one comic ensemble shot, then mostly as a group.

## Supporting cast (background / cameo)
- **Herbert Schoenheit von Vogelsang:** owner of the Beloved Brethren Moratorium, Zurich. Solemn,
  froglike; sallow, unctuous; pulpy hands, eyes like glass buttons; beetle-like; tweed toga, loafers,
  crimson sash, purple **airplane-propeller beanie** that whirs when he's irritated; snuff; plays
  requiems on loop; offers peach tranquilizer gum (ch. 1–2, 7–8). Name means "beauty of the song of
  birds" (ch. 7). Paid by Jory's family to keep Jory near the others (ch. 16). **Great comic cameo.**
- **G. G. Ashwood:** Runciter's telepath scout; square and puffy "like an overweight brick"; mohair
  poncho, apricot felt hat, argyle ski socks, carpet slippers; later birch-bark pantaloons, hemp-rope
  belt, see-through top, train-engineer's hat; manic (ch. 3, 5). Pat's lover and co-conspirator, per
  Pat (ch. 13).
- **"Stanton Mick" (the humanoid bomb):** potbellied, squat, thick-legged; fuchsia pedal-pushers, pink
  yak-fur slippers, snakeskin sleeveless blouse, waist-length dyed white hair with a ribbon; a soft
  nose like a rubber taxi-horn bulb; squeaky "metal bees" voice; floats to the ceiling, rotates and
  explodes (ch. 6).
- **Zoe Wirt:** plump, head like a basketball, narrow dangling legs; old-fashioned spider-silk coat
  ("a bug in a cocoon"); red triangular glasses; glazed, cheerful smile (ch. 4, 6).
- **Raymond Hollis:** rival owner of psi talent; seen only on a vidscreen — a grim blue face, sunken
  eyes like flawed jewels, a face like something dropped and badly reglued; a voice like snakes
  sliding over each other (ch. 7).
- **Mrs. Frick:** Runciter's ancient, trembling secretary with dabs of artificial color (ch. 4).
- **Nina Freede:** Runciter's in-house telepath, reads clients through an earpiece (ch. 4).
- **S. Dole Melipone:** Hollis' top telepath; changes his face every month (ch. 1). Never seen.
- **1939 Des Moines:** Sandy Jespersen (short, fat, red-haired biplane pilot, ch. 10–11); Mr. Bliss
  (mortician in a black suit, smells of Sen-sen, a bigot, ch. 11); the bald pharmacist (Jory, ch. 16);
  the Ubik factory girl in a synthetic ostrich-leather coat ("Myra Laney"?, ch. 16).
- **Len Niggelman:** Prudence Society chairman (ch. 10, 14). Never described.

## Inanimate "characters"
- **Joe's coin-operated front door:** asks for five cents, threatens to sue, outlasts everything else
  that regresses (ch. 3, 10). **Ours:** a recurring gag character.
- **Ubik itself:** changes form with the era — spray can → jar of liver-and-kidney balm → wooden-mold
  bottle of "Elixir of Ubique" → powder → spray can again (ch. 10, 12, 16).
